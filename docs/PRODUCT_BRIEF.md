# Product Brief

## Working product name

Kindlyo

## One-sentence description

Kindlyo is a parent-guided learning platform that helps children ages 5–9
practice kindness, communication, manners, and social confidence through short
interactive stories and real-world missions.

## Problem

Parents want their children to become thoughtful and socially confident, but
teaching these skills can feel repetitive, awkward, inconsistent, or overly
disciplinary.

Existing resources are often:

- Passive videos
- Static worksheets
- Formal etiquette courses
- Generic behavior charts
- Broad social-emotional learning products
- Lists of rules without meaningful practice

## Product opportunity

Create a warm, interactive learning system that lets children practice realistic
social situations before encountering them in real life.

## Core promise

Five-minute lessons that help children feel more confident in everyday social
situations.

## Primary customer

Parents or guardians of children ages 5–9.

## Primary learner

Children ages 5–9.

## Initial use cases

- Greeting people
- Joining conversations
- Listening
- Interrupting appropriately
- Saying please and thank you
- Handling disagreements
- Giving sincere apologies
- Being a guest
- Hosting others
- Sharing public spaces
- Understanding personal boundaries
- Asking adults for help
- Using devices considerately

## Differentiators

- Scenario-based instead of rule-based
- Child and parent experiences work together
- Every lesson includes offline practice
- Context-aware rather than rigid
- Inclusive of different cultures, communication styles, and abilities
- Designed around confidence and consideration
- No advertising to children
- No public social mechanics

## Business model hypothesis

Primary:
- Family subscription

Possible future revenue:
- Annual family memberships
- Situation-specific lesson packs
- Live workshops
- School licensing
- Employer training
- Books and activity cards
- Instructor certification

## Primary validation question

Will families repeatedly use and pay for a short scenario-based social-skills
learning experience?

## MVP success signals

These are internal hypotheses, not established benchmarks.

- At least 50 percent of activated families complete three lessons in week one.
- At least 30 percent return during week two.
- Parents complete at least one offline mission with their child.
- Parents report at least one observed real-world behavior change.
- Some beta families are willing to pay after the trial.
