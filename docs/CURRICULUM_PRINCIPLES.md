# Curriculum Principles

## Educational approach

The application teaches through:

- Short stories
- Decisions
- Consequences
- Explanations
- Rehearsal
- Offline missions
- Spaced review

## Core lesson structure

Every standard lesson contains:

1. Situation
2. Learner decision
3. Outcome
4. Explanation
5. Guided practice
6. Real-world mission
7. Parent coaching prompt
8. Later review

## Content philosophy

Do not teach etiquette as a list of unquestionable rules.

Teach children to consider:

- How another person may feel
- What the situation requires
- Whether safety is involved
- Whether consent is involved
- Cultural or family differences
- Accessibility and communication differences
- Whether urgency changes the appropriate behavior

## Safety principles

Children must understand that they may:

- Say no
- Leave an unsafe interaction
- Interrupt during an emergency
- Ask for help
- Refuse physical contact
- Tell a trusted adult
- Prioritize safety over politeness

## Feedback language

Prefer:

- “Let’s see what might happen.”
- “That could make it harder for Maya to know you want to join.”
- “Another choice may help everyone feel included.”
- “Different situations can need different responses.”

Avoid:

- “Bad manners”
- “You failed”
- “That was rude”
- “Good children always”
- “You should obey”
- Moral judgments about the learner

## Inclusivity

Do not require:

- Eye contact
- Physical touch
- Handshakes
- Forced smiling
- Speaking when an alternative communication method is appropriate

Explain these as context-dependent options.

## Content review status

Each lesson must include:

- `status: draft | reviewed | published`
- `reviewedBy`
- `reviewedAt`
- `culturalNotes`
- `accessibilityNotes`
- `safetyNotes`
